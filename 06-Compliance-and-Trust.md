# 06 — Compliance, Legal & Trust

Read this before writing any product or remedy copy. Two Nigerian regimes apply directly to
what you're planning, and one of them has real teeth.

---

## 1. NAFDAC — much narrower than first assessed

**Update:** you've confirmed APMF sells **honey only, no herbal drugs.** That removes the
heaviest part of the regulatory burden. The Herbal Medicines and Related Products
Advertisement Regulations 2021 — which require NAFDAC to pre-approve every piece of
advertising copy — apply to herbal medicines and dietary supplements. They do not apply to
you as long as you sell no prepared remedies.

**What still applies.** Honey is a packaged food product. Sold commercially it requires
NAFDAC food registration, and the number should appear on every product page. Under the
Pre-Packaged Food (Labelling) Regulation 2022, food products may not carry claims of
medicinal — preventive, alleviative or curative — effect.

**So the rule reduces to one sentence:** *the book and the articles may quote the Qur'an,
the hadith and Ibn al-Qayyim freely; the honey product pages may not claim to treat disease.*

Keep education and commerce on separate pages, and you're clear. The honey chapter of
*Endless Blessings From The Creator* — An-Nahl 16:68–69, *"Hold onto the two cures: honey and
the Qur'an"* — belongs on the **Our Honey** educational page, not on the checkout page.
Quoting revelation is not a product health claim; "cures ulcers" on a buy button is.

**If you ever start selling prepared remedies**, the full herbal regime switches back on and
this section needs rewriting. Flag it to me if that changes.

**Copy that will get you in trouble:**

> ❌ "Cures diabetes" · "Treats infertility" · "Kills cancer cells" ·
> "Eliminates jinn possession" · "Replaces your medication"

**Copy that is defensible:**

> ✅ "The Prophet ﷺ said of black seed: *'In it there is healing for every disease
> except death.'* (Sahih al-Bukhari)"
> ✅ "Traditionally used in prophetic medicine for…"
> ✅ "100% pure honey, no additives, no added sugar"
> ✅ "Some studies have examined honey's antimicrobial properties [link to source]"

**The structural fix.** Separate your content into two clearly distinct categories, visually
and in the navigation:

1. **Educational content** (the Remedies Library, articles) — describing the Islamic textual
   tradition and what researchers have studied. Not selling. Much wider latitude.
2. **Product pages** — describing what is in the jar, its purity and its origin. No disease
   claims whatsoever.

**Required disclaimer**, placed on every remedy and product page in visible text, not buried
in the footer:

> *The information on this page describes traditional prophetic medicine and Islamic
> teaching. It is not medical advice and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent
> any disease. These claims have not been evaluated by NAFDAC. Consult a qualified medical
> practitioner before beginning any treatment, and do not discontinue prescribed medication
> without your doctor's advice.*

**Action:** begin NAFDAC **food** registration for the packaged honey now — it takes months,
not weeks, and the number on your product pages is worth real money in customer trust.
Verify current requirements directly with NAFDAC; regulations are amended.

## 2. NDPA 2023 — data protection

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 took effect on 12 June 2023 and is enforced by the
Nigeria Data Protection Commission. It applies to any organisation processing the personal
data of people in Nigeria. Maximum penalty is 2% of annual gross revenue or ₦10,000,000,
whichever is greater, with criminal liability for wilful violations.

You will be collecting a great deal of sensitive data: empowerment applicants' financial
circumstances, consultation enquiries touching on health, student records, donor details.
Health data is a special category deserving extra care.

**Required:**

- A plain-language **Privacy Policy** stating your identity and contact details, what data
  you collect, why, the legal basis, who you share it with, how long you keep it, and how
  people exercise their rights
- **Consent** that is informed, specific, freely given and unambiguous — an unticked
  checkbox on every form, never pre-ticked
- A **cookie banner** if you use analytics or any non-essential cookies, with genuine
  decline capability
- **Data processing agreements** with your processors: Vercel, Paystack, Cloudinary, your
  email provider
- Reasonable **security measures**: HTTPS, encrypted database, access controls, backups
- A named person responsible for data protection, with an email address published
- A **data breach response plan**
- Check whether APMF meets the threshold for **DPCO registration** with the NDPC and whether
  a Data Protection Officer is required — thresholds are based on volume of data subjects
  processed. Confirm current thresholds on the NDPC website.

**Specific to your case:** get explicit, separate, written consent before publishing any
beneficiary's photograph, name or story. Keep the consent forms. A general form submission
is not consent to be featured on your homepage.

## 3. Charity registration and donation transparency

- **CAC registration** as Incorporated Trustees. Display the registration number in the
  footer and on the Accountability page. Required for a business Paystack account and for a
  `.org.ng` domain.
- **Annual reports**, even simple two-page ones with income, expenditure and beneficiary
  numbers. Publish them as PDFs. Nothing else raises donor confidence as reliably.
- **Zakat administration** — publish your policy: who is eligible under the eight Qur'anic
  categories, how you verify eligibility, and confirmation that 100% of zakat funds reach
  eligible recipients with administration costs met from other sources. Consider having a
  recognised scholar review and endorse the policy, and say so.
- **Separate the accounts.** AHE is a commercial entity, APMF is a charity. Their finances
  must be visibly distinct, and the website should state how AHE profits flow to APMF, if
  they do. Ambiguity here invites suspicion.

## 4. E-commerce terms

You need these published before taking a single payment:

- **Terms of Sale** — pricing, order acceptance, availability
- **Delivery Policy** — zones, timeframes, costs, what happens to failed deliveries
- **Returns & Refunds** — be explicit that food products cannot be returned once opened,
  but that damaged or leaked deliveries will be replaced. Under FCCPA, refusing all returns
  regardless of fault is not enforceable.
- **Course Terms** — withdrawal, refund window, certification conditions
- **Distributor Agreement** — territory, minimum orders, pricing, termination.
  Have a lawyer draft this one; it's a commercial contract, not a web page.
- **Consultation Disclaimer** — what your consultations are and are not, and an explicit
  statement that they do not replace medical care

## 5. Trust signals to build into the design

Nigerian donors and buyers are, reasonably, wary of online payment. Counter that
deliberately:

- CAC number and physical address in the footer
- A real photograph of the office or premises
- Named team members with photographs, not "our team of experts"
- The founder's credentials laid out plainly
- Paystack's secure-checkout badge at the point of payment
- Real testimonials with full names and, where possible, faces
- Bank transfer offered alongside cards
- A phone number that a human answers, displayed prominently
- The empowerment archive — twenty documented monthly cycles is worth more than any badge
- Endorsements from recognised scholars, if you can obtain them

---

**A caveat:** I'm not a lawyer, and Nigerian regulations change. Everything above should be
verified with a Nigerian legal or regulatory adviser before launch. The NAFDAC point in
particular is worth paying for proper advice on.
